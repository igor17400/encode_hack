Raw intraoperative footage from a 30-degree laparoscope during gallbladder surgery, recorded in standard definition by a laparoscopic video tower: soft, slightly muddy image, muted colours, mild compression smearing and faint noise in the dark areas. Circular black endoscope mask, cropped at the top and bottom of a 16:9 frame. A single light source on the lens axis: nearby tissue is bright and the background falls quickly into darkness. Wet surfaces show tiny pinpoint specular sparkles; smooth convex surfaces and metal shafts show large blown-out white highlights. Overall magenta and purple-pink colour cast. The camera is handheld by an assistant, very close to the tissue, drifting slightly and refocusing now and then.

The distended gallbladder fills much of the upper left of the frame: a mauve-violet wall with a fine network of thin red vessels under a glossy surface. The liver is smooth, glossy and deep maroon, almost black in shadow. Dull yellow-orange fat lies along the lower edge, slightly out of focus. Everything in view is inside the abdomen.

The dissected tissue around the gallbladder neck looks raw and matte. It is unevenly stained a dark reddish-brown, flat and blotchy as if soaked into the tissue, with irregular brownish burnt edges along earlier dissection lines. The staining is part of the tissue surface itself, never separate drops or beads. Everything glistens with moisture.

Only two instruments are in view the whole time. A fenestrated grasper enters from the left edge and holds the gallbladder neck, pulling it gently left and down; it stays almost perfectly still. A hook electrode enters from the upper right: a thick grey insulated shaft ending in a small metal L-shaped tip only a few millimetres long.

At the start, the area between the gallbladder neck and the liver is already cleared: a dark open window shows the near-black liver behind it. Exactly two separate pale pinkish-white bands run into the gallbladder neck, a wider one below and a thinner one above. The lower gallbladder still lies flat against the liver.

Over about six seconds, the grasper lifts the gallbladder neck slightly up and away from the liver while the tip of the hook stays in contact with the tissue, moving in short strokes along the junction between gallbladder and liver and peeling the lower gallbladder free. Thin tissue tents under the tip and springs back. The hook then withdraws straight back along its shaft and leaves the frame, and the view holds for the last two seconds.

At the end, beneath the lifted lower gallbladder a strip of smooth, raw, whitish-pink tissue on the liver side is visible with brownish burnt edges, while the upper gallbladder stays attached. The two bands and the dark window remain unchanged.
